# ANALYSIS — reference-game motion grammar (Phase A)

Evidence: `rec-krillion-dive.webm`, `rec-krillion-party.webm`, `rec-jinxed.webm`, `rec-playdaily.webm` + 17 stills in this folder, plus prior first-hand play sessions (documented in `../01_REFERENCE_GAME_ANALYSIS.md`). All study of composition/motion/pacing only — no asset reuse.

## Krillion — The Daily Dive (the bar)

1. **The scene IS the scoreboard.** The vertical ocean is a real depth ruler: tier bands (-100m/-200m/…) are painted INTO the world, and the reveal is diegetic — your answer tag physically detaches and sinks past the markers to its tier's depth. No abstract results table. This is the single strongest idea in the genre.
2. **Multi-layer parallax, always moving.** Far: gradient bands + light rays. Mid: kelp + fish silhouettes. Near: rising bubbles. Between prompts the camera *descends* and every layer moves at a different rate — the world has depth even while idle.
3. **Continuous ambient life.** Fish schools cross the frame, bubbles rise, kelp sways, the boat bobs at the surface. Nothing is static; the game feels inhabited between inputs.
4. **One unified post-processing idea: CRT.** Scanlines + subtle chromatic aberration on the logo + vignette + a "zap" flash on transitions. Cheap per-frame cost, huge identity payoff — it makes simple pixel art feel authored.
5. **Diegetic HUD.** DEPTH = your score, always ticking; sonar-ping timer ring; progress squares as flares. Nothing is an abstract widget.
6. **Feedback as texture.** Input echo ("cerulean: no echo. try again") is styled as sonar readout; a mascot comments ("blub!"); every state transition is animated, never swapped.
7. **Party lobby status-as-identity:** avatar tiers (KRILL→EVIL) are unlockable rank displays; a giant bobbing pufferfish anchors the screen; chunky pixel chips for every control.

## Jinxed
Flat pastel "potion lab" theme, minimal motion — the mechanic (type many, duplicates jinx) carries it. Reveal is a plain list. Confirms: without a living scene, a rarity game relies entirely on social energy.

## Playdaily clone
Utilitarian cards, no motion, no metaphor. Reads as a quiz form. Best counter-example: proves the Krillion feel is the art/motion investment, not the genre.

## Comparison vs RAREBIRD (current, v13 + art pack)

| Dimension | Krillion | RAREBIRD today | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene-as-scoreboard | depth ruler painted in world | altitude ruler is a thin side gutter | **big** — our metaphor is as good, its staging isn't |
| Parallax layers | 3+ always moving | static gradients + drifting emoji clouds (art=1: 2 static SVG layers) | **big** |
| Ambient life | fish/bubbles/kelp | 1 drifting cloud per theme; twinkles static | **big** |
| Reveal choreography | tag sinks diegetically, tier card slams | rows pop in bottom-up | medium |
| Post-processing identity | CRT scanlines/vignette/zap | none | medium |
| HUD diegesis | depth=score, sonar timer | numeric timer ring + feather counter | small |
| Transitions | zap flashes between states | instant screen swaps | small |

**Conclusion:** our altitude metaphor is structurally equal to Krillion's depth; we lose on *staging*. The bold layer must make the world (1) a continuous vertical journey with visible altitude bands, (2) alive at all times, (3) choreographed at the reveal, (4) dressed with one unified post-processing idea.
